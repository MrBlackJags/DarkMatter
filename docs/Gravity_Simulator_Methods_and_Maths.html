1. Classical Gravitational Dynamics & The N-Body Problem

In an isolated system containing N discrete point masses, classical gravitational interactions are governed by Newton's law of universal gravitation. For each pair of bodies i and j with gravitational masses mi and mj located at position vectors r⃗i and r⃗j, the gravitational force exerted on body i by body j is formulated as:

F→ij = − G mimj |rj−ri|3 (rj−ri) = G mimj |rij|2 r^ij

where r⃗ij = r⃗j − r⃗i, and r̂ij = r⃗ij / |r⃗ij| is the unit displacement vector directed from body i toward body j. Applying the principle of linear superposition across all N − 1 perturbing bodies yields the total gravitational acceleration a⃗i:

a→i = d2ri dt2 = ∑j≠iN G mj(rj−ri) |rj−ri|3

1.1 Singularities and Plummer Gravitational Softening

As mutual inter-particle distance approaches zero (rij → 0), the classical acceleration exhibits a 1 / r2 singularity. In continuous numerical integration, small time steps cannot resolve the infinite force spikes during close hyperbolic encounters, resulting in spurious unphysical ejections (numerical scattering). To preserve physical fidelity and maintain algorithmic stability, the simulator introduces the Plummer Softening Kernel parameter ε:

a→i = ∑j≠iN G mj(rj−ri) (|rj−ri|2+ε2)3/2

In our calibrated simulator engine, ε2 = 25.0 px2 (ε = 5.0 px), ensuring smooth force transitions when point particles traverse close proximity while converging precisely to the pure Newtonian regime when r ≫ ε.

1.2 Fundamental Conservation Laws in Hamiltonian Mechanics

The unconstrained N-body gravitational system constitutes a conservative dynamical system described by the Hamiltonian H:

H(r,p) = T+V = ∑i=1N |pi|22mi − ∑i=1N ∑j>iN Gmimj (|rij|2+ε2)1/2
Theorem: Invariants of Motion
For any isolated system with isotropic spatial symmetries:
• Total Linear Momentum: P⃗ = ∑ mi v⃗i = constant
• Total Angular Momentum: L⃗ = ∑ mi (r⃗i × v⃗i) = constant
• Total Mechanical Energy: E = T + V = constant

1.3 Universal Anchoring Mechanics (Fixed vs Dynamic Bodies)

When a celestial body is flagged as an anchored core (b.isCore = true), it acts as an infinite-inertia fixed potential well:

2. Numerical Integration Schemes & Symplectic Solvers

2.1 Instability of Forward Euler Integration

The simplest numerical discretization of Newton's second law is the first-order Explicit Forward Euler scheme:

r(t+Δt) = r(t)+v(t)Δt , v(t+Δt) = v(t)+a(r(t))Δt

Forward Euler is non-symplectic and mathematically unstable for orbital mechanics. In phase space, the Jacobian determinant exceeds unity (det(J) > 1), causing the numerical orbits of circular trajectories to continuously spiral outward, systematically pumping artificial energy into the system and destroying orbital closure within a few revolutions.

2.2 The Symplectic Velocity Verlet / Leapfrog Scheme

To guarantee bounded energy oscillations and eliminate secular orbital drift, the DarkMatter simulator implements the Symplectic Velocity Verlet (Kick-Drift-Kick Leapfrog) solver:

Kick Step 1:  v(t+12Δt) = v(t) + 12a(t)Δt Drift Step:  r(t+Δt) = r(t) + v(t+12Δt)Δt Force Re-evaluation:  a(t+Δt) = f(r(t+Δt)) Kick Step 2:  v(t+Δt) = v(t+12Δt) + 12a(t+Δt)Δt
Mathematical Properties of Velocity Verlet
1. Second-Order Accuracy: Local truncation error is O(Δt3); global error is O(Δt2).
2. Time-Reversible: Inverting the sign of Δt exactly traces the trajectory backward in time.
3. Symplectic (Phase Space Volume Preservation): The mapping preserves the Poincaré invariant 2-form ω = ∑ dqi ∧ dpi, exactly conserving a shadow Hamiltonian H̃ = H + O(Δt2) with zero long-term secular energy dissipation.
